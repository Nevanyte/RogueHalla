# Quest return against normal farming

The target is a **25–50% premium for a well-chosen quest** after travel, objective time and direct input cost. One normal camp-equivalent (CE) is a nearby normal clear paying 12 stacks. When comparing two different stats, 12 stacks is an accounting unit. The preferred-stat duel measures give a separate view of combat value.

## Route measurement

The observer reads the focal RaidBot's current goal after each delivered decision, using the ordinary hosted policy and command path. The same player can switch between quests, normal camps and other goals. It records seconds with each quest goal selected while standing and gates open, beginning before the quest was accepted. For a paid quest, the approach and the objective/return are split at the first acceptance. Time on a different goal is excluded. Goal time on a quest that never paid still contributes to the cost per payout. The final leg is capped at settlement, and identifiable normal camp credits earned in the covered legs are deducted. A simultaneous elite and normal clear is left unassigned.

Each second is priced at the focal faction's observed rate of normal clear credit outside quest goals in the same five-minute age band (0–5, 5–10, 10+). This includes walking, recovery, player fights, idle time and elite detours outside quest goals. It prices the alternatives these seats actually took on these nearby maps. A specific nearby camp can have a different return, especially for a player who targets one stat. Timing uses the 25 ms authoritative raid clock. Reported route medians are successful payouts. Expected cost is the mean route spend per reward including abandoned or failed work. The one eligible focal seat plays against seven true fill RaidBots. Live human contention, slotted spell displacement and deaths following later choices are outside this estimate.

For permanent stack payouts, `V = (received stacks − sacrificed stack equivalents) / 12`. The premium is `V / route cost − 1`. If the player pays 20 stacks that were useful, all 20 count as input. If they pay by surrendering a weapon, charge that particular weapon's opportunity value. For a short-lived buff, travel utility and combat insurance, the right alternative is the useful result it can deliver **in that situation**.

## Design valuation for situational rewards

The reference examples below price saved work at **1.5 normal clears/minute**, so saving 20, 40 or 60 seconds is worth 0.5, 1.0 or 1.5 CE. These are design scenarios. Their benefit estimates require the stated circumstance, and the trace cannot show how often a human would see it or its counterfactual outcome. Where the payoff is an immediate combat swing, the controlled comparison against a preferred camp is a separate benchmark. It overrepresents committed equal-kit fights and underrepresents terrain, movement, teams and opponents with different weapons.

|---|---|
| The Warden | A cage that closes a melee approach and buys a credible 40-second escape/recovery route is worth 1 CE. A shooter with a clear lane can keep firing through it. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Hold the Fort | Turret damage against players and mobs can replace repeated approaches in a team fight or help several camp pulls. Budget 1.5–2 CE if it collectively saves 60–80 seconds, with lower value when the player cannot stay in range. The stock field recorded casts, and the committed duel gave weak value versus one preferred camp. |
| Bounty Hunter | A Hook that closes one disengage plus 20–40 seconds of reacquisition is worth 0.5–1 CE. It needs a foe who would otherwise escape or a team that can cash in the pull. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Smuggler's Run | Shoulder Charge can save 0.5–1 CE when an escape or entry replaces 20–40 seconds of recovery or routing. The stock cast count establishes use. |
| Two Banners | Warhorn needs enemies in range to turn its taunt into damage and guard. Price one successful crowd use at 1–1.5 CE if it avoids 40–60 seconds of retreat or lost route. Stock RaidBot holds it without a cast rule. |
| Ember Walk | Spell Shield is an insurance choice when one incoming important spell would force retreat. Price such a block at 0.5–1 CE for 20–40 seconds saved. The stock policy does not cast it. |
| The Burning Crown | Receives one Ember Imp stack per whole second carried, capped at 48 stacks or 4 same-stat CE. Carry burns 1% max HP per second and delays other work. At the cap, the duel confirms strong committed power. Bot route cost and public carrier pressure remain unmeasured. |
| Let Him Cook | Chef's Kiss buffs the least-developed of the eligible positive-base stats by 20%. Its stack equivalent depends on the actual chosen stat and current sheet. The committed duel showed no useful edge for its tested builds. It has no spell slot cost. |
| Elite Slayer | Smite's 220 burst matters at an elite threshold. Conservatively price 20–40 seconds of accelerated or recovered elite work at 0.5–1 CE, on top of expected loot only if the cast actually changes who earns it. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Tuber Eats | Breaker's Helm converts a fifth of physical Bow/Axe weapon damage to true. It benefits physical attacks into enough armor and has no spell slot cost. The ten-build duel found an overall advantage vs an unaugmented kit and a loss vs one preferred camp. Weapon match decides value. |
| Barrow Run | A WallBuster detour cut of 20–40 seconds is worth 0.5–1 CE. Position changes affect nearby routes for both sides. |
| The Bargain | Before reallocation quality, the fair-value stack branch is 20 stacks paid and 28 received. Net 8/12 CE. The spare weapon branch receives 28 stacks and subtracts what replacement or weapon flexibility is worth to that build. |
| A Cruel Joke | Swap-Stick pays when ten seconds on the wrong weapon disrupts the target or lends the player a critical counter. Price a prevented 40–60-second regroup or retreat at 1–1.5 CE. Stock RaidBot does not cast it. |
| A Bone to Pick | Reusable Bolas can deny an escape or secure a team's follow-up on a rooted target. Price the prevented 20–40-second pursuit at 0.5–1 CE. Committed mirrors give little value for a root that cannot replace movement. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Feed or Hunt | The permanent grant is six Ember Imp stacks, or 0.5 same-stat CE, before later farming dividends and delivery costs. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Rime Hunt | Tornado is a placement and team follow-up choice. A pull that eliminates one 40–60-second reposition is worth 1–1.5 CE. The committed mirror is a weak proxy. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Watchtowers | Scout Falcon is useful if reconnaissance avoids a 20–40-second failed approach, worth 0.5–1 CE. Only one stock claim supports its route sample. |
| Whispers in the Brush | A Lantern Ward that keeps a route useful across two 20-second detours saves 1 CE. Works when the vision creates a decision. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Even Skeletons Get Cold | Warm Coat is a no-slot permanent. Avoiding cumulative 20–60 seconds of chill and bog slowdown over a zone rotation is worth 0.5–1.5 CE. Actual savings depend on opposition and route exposure. |
| Lost Brother | Split draughts have different windows. The active BlindRage helping a player commit and StillMind avoiding an interruption can save 0.5–1.5 CE collectively, after accounting for two slots or a draught left on the ground. The tested direct duel had an edge but lost to one preferred camp. |
| Pilgrim's Path | A Cleanse that lets a rooted or stunned fighter avoid a 20–40-second disengage is worth 0.5–1 CE. Lower value with no control effects present. |
| Gatecrasher | Rebirth Egg is conditional on a real death and a hatchable position. Replacing 60–120 seconds of lost raid time values one successful use at 1.5–3 CE, discounted by the chance it activates. Bot quest path unobserved. |
| Letters | A return teleport replacing 20–60 seconds on a planned double-back is worth 0.5–1.5 CE. It needs a useful anchor; there is only one stock route sample. |
| Stay A While | A Stasis that absorbs an otherwise decisive burst is worth 0.5–1.5 CE if it prevents 20–60 seconds of forced disengagement. Both the inability to attack and the spell slot have costs. |
| Trade Up | Cash-outs grant 10, 22 or 32 Penitent stacks (0.83, 1.83 or 2.67 CE) without surrendering a stat or taking a spell slot. Earlier exits carry less travel exposure. The stage-three share is reflected in RaidBot's planner. |
