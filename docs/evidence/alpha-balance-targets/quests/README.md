# Quest return against normal farming

The target is a **25–50% premium for a well-chosen quest** after travel, objective time and direct input cost. One normal camp-equivalent (CE) is a nearby normal clear paying 12 stacks. When comparing two different stats, 12 stacks is an accounting unit. The preferred-stat duel measures give a separate view of combat value.

## Route measurement

The observer reads the focal RaidBot's current goal after each delivered decision, using the ordinary hosted policy and command path. The same player can switch between quests, normal camps and other goals. It records seconds with each quest goal selected while standing and gates open, beginning before the quest was accepted. For a paid quest, the approach and the objective/return are split at the first acceptance. Time on a different goal is excluded. Goal time on a quest that never paid still contributes to the cost per payout. The final leg is capped at settlement, and identifiable normal camp credits earned in the covered legs are deducted. A simultaneous elite and normal clear is left unassigned.

Each second is priced at the focal faction's observed rate of normal clear credit outside quest goals in the same raid-age band (0–5, 5–10, 10+). This includes walking, recovery, player fights, idle time and elite detours outside quest goals. It prices the alternatives these seats actually took on these nearby maps. A specific nearby camp can have a different return, especially for a player who targets one stat. Timing uses the 25 ms authoritative raid clock. Reported route medians are successful payouts. Expected cost is the mean route spend per reward including abandoned or failed work. The one eligible focal seat plays against seven true fill RaidBots. Live human contention, slotted spell displacement and deaths following later choices are outside this estimate.

For permanent stack payouts, `V = (received stacks − sacrificed stack equivalents) / 12`. The premium is `V / route cost − 1`. If the player pays 20 stacks that were useful, all 20 count as input. If they pay by surrendering a weapon, charge that particular weapon's opportunity value. For a short-lived buff, travel utility and combat insurance, the right alternative is the useful result it can deliver **in that situation**.

## Payout and objective changes

The baseline adds matched route instrumentation to all 80 frozen stock maps. Its observer leaves all 80 terminal state hashes unchanged. The 32-stack sensitivity run used 20 of those maps, with two original and two held-out focal seats per faction and Trade Up available on every map. The final rules were replayed on the 10 held-out seeds, two per faction. All treatment sets use the same frozen normal-farming rates and otherwise follow the corresponding local alpha build. The variant comparison measures the resulting routes in those builds.

Trade Up's HP grant should be compared with a nearby clear paying a useful HP stat. In committed equal-build duels against **two clears of the opponent's preferred stat**, the 29-stack grant scored 35% over 60 seat exchanges. The 54-stack grant scored 95% over the same cells. That is a separate stress case for builds better served by specialized farming. [Trade Up combat screen](trade_up_duel_screen.csv)

Of the permanent rewards with field times, **Let Him Cook** remains the clearest low-return choice for generic direct stat growth. Its mean route costs 1.945 CE, so it needs 2.43–2.92 CE for the requested premium. If its weakest stat receives about 20 units out of 100 as damage or health, that is about 20 comparable stacks (1.67 CE) before dividends. If it awards movement, the committed duel misses its benefit. At the reference farm rate a pure 20% speed bonus would need to save about 97–117 seconds of movement for the target to hold, roughly 10–12 minutes of affected baseline movement. **Tuber Eats** needs approximately 1.24–1.49 CE (mean route 0.99 CE), so the armor bypass is a worthwhile pick for enough Bow/Axe time into armor, rather than a generally competitive passive. **Warm Coat** needs 0.91–1.09 CE, or 36–44 seconds saved, which favors sustained chill and bog exposure. **The Burning Crown** could overpay on a clean full stack run. It lacks a field executor, and the full carry burns up to roughly 48% max HP and broadcasts the holder, so the net result is unmeasured.

A general stat floor based on elapsed quest time would reward inefficient approaches and long stalls. A floor based on objective count still mixes unrelated risks and utilities. One ward could outperform a pure-stat grant for a coordinated group, while a long information route in a quiet zone could offer almost no help. Feed or Hunt has a known stack payout and a materially low return even at the low end of its designed travel. That supports a fixed change there without assigning free stats to a spell whose payoff is not adequately observed.

The Feed or Hunt damage screen advanced from a 5% to a 30% score against one deliberately preferred normal camp. Against the equal base it held a 100% score and raised mean surviving-health margin from 7.0 to 12.3 percentage points. Each case has 60 seat exchanges at 180 preexisting stacks with Wishes. The screen checks the actual payout via the reward installer. Its preferred-stat comparator remains stricter than the design route's direct same-stat return. ## Design valuation for situational rewards

The reference examples below use **1.5 normal clears/minute**, near the observed pooled 0–5 minute outside-quest rate of 1.58 (0.94–2.12 by faction), so saving 20, 40 or 60 seconds is worth 0.5, 1.0 or 1.5 CE. These are design scenarios. Their benefit estimates require the stated circumstance, and the trace cannot show how often a human would see it or its counterfactual outcome. Where the payoff is an immediate combat swing, the controlled comparison against a preferred camp is a separate benchmark. It overrepresents committed equal-kit fights and underrepresents terrain, movement, teams and opponents with different weapons.

The cost ranges below are the middle 50% of observed successful stock route costs, or a single marked field sample. Unplayed paths use the authored time ranges in the analysis script at the same 1.5 CE/minute. These ranges reflect active time and typical approach under the stated objective. Warden prices the special encounter, bounty includes pursuit, crown includes the time to reach a full grant, Rime subtracts one matching normal clear, and Gatecrasher excludes time spent on other work while the lock runs. These rows cannot price human death risk or opposition reliably.

## Reproduction

Build the observer with `cargo build --release -p rh-headless --bin quest_balance`. At the stock rules with the route observer applied, run `target/release/quest_balance 1 81 > stock-routes.jsonl`. Run the final binary once per held-out seed with `target/release/quest_balance SEED SEED_PLUS_ONE`. The held-out seeds are 8, 16, 19, 28, 37, 40, 59, 70, 72 and 76. Pass the outputs to `python3 tools/balance-lab/quest_economy.py tuned-routes-*.jsonl --rate-traces stock-routes.jsonl --output route_29_treatment.csv`. To check observer neutrality, add `--baseline` with the original stock trace and use the stock instrumented capture as the analysis input.
